Like the deepening autumn, the sky these days is so high and clear. Whenever the seasons change, I find myself habitually whispering:

"Ah, the weather is so nice."

But as I paused to reflect, I realized that those words weren't merely about the clear, cool air. Just as the beauty of the seasons arrives without fail, I wonder how often we let these fleeting moments slip right past us. Like missing a four-leaf clover while staring at a bed of three-leaf ones, happiness always seems to slip right past us.

In the review section of a critically failed film, someone once left this single line:

"I loved it so much because I was with you."

Though the film received harsh reviews from the world and failed terribly at the box office, for that one person, it had become an unforgettable scene. Not because of the movie, but because of the person sitting right beside them. I wonder how deeply we truly cherish the present moment.

> The flower sprouts piercing through the soil when spring arrives, 
> The snow dusting gently upon bare winter branches, 
> The glowing crimson sunset of autumn, 
> And the dazzling, brilliant sunlight of summer— 
> how beautiful they all are, yet we so often live forgetting it.

**'When the time comes, it arrives naturally without being asked.'**

In the face of pain and trials, we often lose track of time itself. Yet, despite everything, the seasons return without fail. Looking at this unchanging beauty, I am reminded of that person who is no longer by my side. And a sudden thought crosses my mind:

Just as I feel the beauty of this season right now, wouldn't that person wish for my happiness too? Perhaps that is all they ever wanted for me.

Lately, I've been listening frequently to "Care," a track from the new album Live Archive 2025: Fortune Teller by Sim Gyuseon (심규선), also known as Lucia — an artist I have truly loved for a very long time. While listening to that song, a prayer suddenly surfaced in my mind:

> Glória Patri, et Fílio, et Spirítui Sancto. 
> Sicut erat in princípio, et nunc, et semper, et in sæcula sæculórum. Amen.

> (Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.)

In the lyric, **"I will love you, now and always, forever,"** I heard the echo of the Doxology. Perhaps it was because of my daily habit of praying as a Catholic, or perhaps because of the even deeper emotion captured in that live recording. It was likely both.

Sometimes, when I look at the pain and sadness of those around me, I find myself thinking that even the angels can feel rather indifferent to our suffering. Perhaps because neither God nor the angels can shield us completely from every harm, He left us with the commandment to "love one another." Meaning, in the end, we are meant to become angels for each other.
